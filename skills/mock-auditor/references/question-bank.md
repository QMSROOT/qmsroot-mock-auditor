# Question Bank and Evidence Trails (optional inspiration)

Use only for the topic and framework agreed with the participant. These are prompts to adapt, not a script, checklist, sequence or limit on follow-up. Ask about the process and the status of the case first; pursue specific evidence only when it is useful. Clause numbers are deliberately omitted where they could be misattributed. Paraphrase requirements and check exact citations as described in reference-status.md.

"Common weak points" are areas worth exploring. They are not findings, and they do not presume a failure.

---

## Cross-framework processes

### CAPA / corrective action
- How does an issue become a corrective action here, who decides, and on what basis?
- Can you walk me through one recent case from trigger to closure, as far as it has progressed?
- How was the cause established, and what supports it?
- How was effectiveness defined, and when will (or did) you check it?
- How do you decide that a formal CAPA is *not* needed, and where is that recorded?
Common weak points: cause stated without supporting evidence; effectiveness equated with implementation; no rationale for proportionality decisions.

### Complaints and feedback
- What counts as a complaint in your procedure? How would you treat [borderline example relevant to the case]?
- Walk me through one complaint from intake to its current status.
- Where complaints may have regulatory reporting implications, how is that question raised, by whom and when? (Do not decide reportability.)
- How do complaint trends feed into analysis, risk and improvement?
Common weak points: narrow complaint definition; unclear reporting decision path; trending not linked to action.

### Document control and records
- How do people know they are using the current version at point of use?
- How is a change reviewed, approved and assessed for impact?
- How are obsolete documents kept out of use, and how are external documents kept current?
- How long are records kept, and what is that based on?
Common weak points: uncontrolled copies; no impact assessment; retention not justified.

### Supplier / externally provided processes and products
- How is supplier control scaled to the risk of what they supply?
- For a key supplier, how were they selected, and when and how were they last evaluated?
- How would you learn about a change the supplier makes?
Common weak points: controls not proportionate to risk; change notification not addressed; re-evaluation overdue.

### Training and competence
- How are competence needs determined for this role?
- For a person doing the task in the case, how was competence established?
- How do you judge whether training worked?
Common weak points: attendance treated as competence; no evaluation of effectiveness.

### Internal audit
Records to sample: audit programme/schedule, one recent audit plan and report, a finding with its linked CAPA and verification, auditor qualification/competence records.
- How is the programme planned, and how does risk or past results shape it?
- How is auditor independence ensured?
- Take one finding: how was it responded to and verified?
Common weak points: programme not risk-informed; closure without verification.

### Management review
- What went into the last review, and where is each input addressed?
- What decisions and actions came out, and what is their status now?
Common weak points: inputs missing; outputs without owners or follow-up.

## ISO 13485 / medical devices (incl. MDSAP)

- **Design and development:** trace one requirement from input through output, verification and validation; how design changes were assessed for impact, including on the risk file.
- **Risk management:** how risk management is applied across the lifecycle and linked to complaints, CAPA and design changes.
- **Production and process validation:** which processes cannot be fully verified afterwards, how they were validated, and what triggers revalidation.
- **Monitoring and measuring equipment:** what happens when equipment is found out of calibration, and how product measured with it is assessed.
- **Post-market surveillance:** what data sources feed PMS, how often, and how results reach risk, clinical evaluation and CAPA (framework-dependent; e.g. EU MDR).
- **MDSAP specifics:** which participating jurisdictions apply to the exercise; how country-specific requirements (e.g. adverse event reporting, registration, labelling) are identified, assigned and evidenced, kept distinct from ISO 13485 requirements.

Example trails: complaint → investigation → reporting decision → CAPA → risk file → effectiveness → management review. Supplier nonconformity → supplier corrective action → re-evaluation → incoming verification change.

## ISO 9001 (quality management systems, general)

Confirm the edition the organisation is certified to (and any amendments) before discussing requirements.

- **Context and interested parties:** how the organisation determined relevant internal and external issues and interested parties' requirements, and how these are reviewed.
- **Leadership and quality policy:** how top management shows accountability; how the policy and objectives are communicated and used.
- **Risks and opportunities:** how risks and opportunities for the QMS were determined, what actions were planned, and how their effectiveness is evaluated.
- **Quality objectives:** how objectives are set, measured and followed up.
- **Operational control:** how customer requirements are determined and reviewed before commitment; how changes to requirements are handled.
- **Monitoring and measurement / customer satisfaction:** what is monitored, how results are analysed and acted on.
- **Nonconforming outputs and corrective action:** how nonconforming outputs are identified and controlled; how corrective action is decided and evaluated.
- **Continual improvement:** how improvement opportunities are selected and pursued.
Common weak points: risk-based thinking documented but not used in decisions; objectives not measurable; customer satisfaction data collected but not analysed.

Example trail: customer complaint → nonconforming output control → corrective action → objective/KPI review → management review.

## GMP (sector- and jurisdiction-dependent)

First clarify sector (e.g. medicinal products, APIs, sterile manufacture, medical gases, cosmetics, food) and the GMP regime that applies (e.g. EU GMP guidelines, US cGMP regulations, PIC/S guide, ICH guidelines such as Q7/Q9/Q10 where relevant). Do not import requirements from another regime.

- **Pharmaceutical quality system:** how quality risk management and management review operate within the quality system.
- **Deviations and CAPA:** how deviations are classified, investigated and linked to batch disposition and CAPA.
- **Change control:** how changes are assessed for impact on product quality, validation status and regulatory filings.
- **Batch documentation and release:** walk through review of one batch record to release; who releases, on what basis.
- **Laboratory controls / out-of-specification results:** how an unexpected result is investigated before any retest or invalidation.
- **Data integrity:** how records are kept attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate (and complete, consistent, enduring, available); how audit trails are reviewed.
- **Qualification and validation:** equipment qualification, process validation, cleaning validation — current status and what triggers re-evaluation.
- **Contamination control / environmental monitoring (where relevant, e.g. sterile):** how the control strategy is defined, monitored and trended; what happens on an excursion.
- **Suppliers and outsourced activities:** qualification of material suppliers and contract manufacturers/labs; quality agreements.
- **Product quality review:** how periodic product reviews are compiled and used.
Common weak points: deviations closed without root cause or impact assessment; retesting before investigation; audit trails not reviewed; change control not linked to validation.

Example trails: OOS result → investigation → batch disposition → CAPA → trend review. Deviation → impact assessment → change control → revalidation.
