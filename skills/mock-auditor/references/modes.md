# Framework modes

Apply only the mode the user chose. Do not import requirements from another framework. Check `reference-status.md` before stating any edition, date, clause number or timeline as fact.

## ISO 13485 (medical device QMS)
- Default mode. Use the edition the user states; if unstated, refer to "ISO 13485" and note edition assumptions as unverified.
- Typical audit types: internal, certification (stage 1/2), surveillance, recertification, Notified Body–linked audits.
- Emphasise documented procedures, records, risk-based approach across processes, and links between processes (e.g. complaints → CAPA → risk management → management review).
- Clause numbers only if verified or taken from the user's copy.

## MDSAP (by jurisdiction)
- Ask which participating jurisdictions apply (e.g. Australia, Brazil, Canada, Japan, USA), unless stated.
- Audit ISO 13485 requirements plus the jurisdiction-specific requirements for the selected jurisdictions. Keep them distinct in questions and in the report.
- Typical topics where jurisdictions differ: adverse event / vigilance reporting, device registration or licensing, labelling, advisory notices/recalls, distribution records.
- Do not decide reportability or regulatory status; ask how the organisation decides and who decides.
- Grading: MDSAP grading per `grading.md` for established nonconformities.
- The MDSAP audit model follows a defined process sequence; the participant's process choice may be one of those processes. Do not quote the audit model's task numbers unless verified.

## ISO 9001 (general QMS)
- Use the edition the user states (e.g. 2015 or a later edition). If they state an edition you cannot verify, use their stated context and say requirement details will be paraphrased from general QMS principles and should be checked against their copy. Do not assert publication or transition status.
- If the organisation makes medical devices, flag the usual relevance of ISO 13485 once, then continue as chosen.
- Typical emphasis: context, interested parties, risks and opportunities, objectives, operational control, customer satisfaction, nonconforming outputs, improvement.
- Grading: typical major/minor (see `grading.md`).

## FDA QMSR-style inspection
- Frame as a practice inspection in the style of a US FDA inspection under the Quality Management System Regulation (21 CFR 820), which incorporates ISO 13485 by reference. Details of the regulation's scope, effective date and inspection approach are **unverified** here; do not state them as fact.
- Behave like an investigator: request records early, follow the "show me" approach, focus on complaints, CAPA, design changes, production controls, MDR-related decisions and management responsibility.
- Do not decide whether an event is reportable under 21 CFR 803; ask how they decide.
- Report: use "inspectional observations" style for established nonconformities instead of major/minor; do not predict a Form 483, warning letter or classification.

## GMP (sector- and regime-specific)
- Clarify sector and regime (e.g. EU GMP, US cGMP, PIC/S). Do not apply medical-device requirements.
- Classifications (critical/major/other) only if the user names the regime and, ideally, supplies the definitions.
