# CAPA Methodology (optional CAPA resource)

Use only when the agreed process is CAPA or corrective/preventive action, or when another topic's trail genuinely reaches CAPA. This is background for proportionate questioning, not a checklist, a required method or a limit on follow-up. Paraphrase requirements; check exact citations as described in reference-status.md.

---

## What the auditor is trying to understand

A CAPA process is judged on whether it reliably turns information about problems into proportionate, evidence-based action that works. The useful questions are about decisions and their basis, not about whether a form is filled in.

Keep these distinct throughout the conversation:
- **Correction:** action on the nonconforming item or immediate situation (e.g. rework, quarantine, replacing a label).
- **Corrective action:** action on the cause, to prevent recurrence.
- **Preventive action:** action on the cause of a *potential* problem, to prevent occurrence. Some frameworks and editions treat this differently (e.g. ISO 9001 integrates it into risk-based thinking); confirm the applicable framework and edition rather than assuming.
- **Proposal, expected result, observed result:** a planned action, what it is meant to achieve, and what evidence shows it achieved. Do not treat one as another.

## Lifecycle stages and what to explore

These stages are typical, not mandatory. Organisations may combine, rename or sequence them differently; assess what they actually do against what applies to them.

### 1. Inputs and triggers
- Which data sources can feed CAPA (complaints, nonconformities, internal and external audits, deviations, service, returns, process and product monitoring, trends, PMS, supplier issues, management review)?
- How are these sources monitored and analysed for issues that need action, including trends that no single event would reveal?

### 2. Entry decision and proportionality
- Who decides whether an issue needs a formal CAPA, using what criteria, and is the decision recorded with a rationale?
- Is the effort scaled to risk? A low-risk isolated event may reasonably be handled by correction plus a documented decision not to open a CAPA.
- Not opening a CAPA is not a nonconformity in itself; the question is whether the decision was reasoned and consistent with the procedure.

### 3. Containment and correction
- What immediate action protected product, patients, users or customers while the cause was investigated?
- Was the scope of affected product, batches, sites or suppliers considered, including product already distributed where relevant?
- Where regulatory reporting or field action could be relevant, how was that question raised, and who must decide it? (Do not decide reportability or field action.)

### 4. Investigation and cause
- How was the cause established, and what evidence supports it?
- Tools such as 5 Whys, fishbone/Ishikawa, fault tree, is/is-not analysis or data analysis are all acceptable; **no single technique is required.** Ask how the chosen approach suited the problem.
- Is the depth of investigation proportionate to the risk and complexity?
- Was the possibility of multiple or contributing causes considered?
- Does the stated cause explain the observed facts, or is it a restatement of the symptom?
- "Human error" or "operator error" may be a genuine finding, but explore why the error was possible (design, instructions, workload, training, interfaces) before accepting it as the end point.

### 5. Action planning
- Do the actions address the established cause rather than only the symptom?
- Were the actions assessed for their own risk and for side effects, including on validated processes, design, risk files, documents, suppliers and regulatory filings?
- Are owners and target dates set? There is no universal deadline; assess whether timing is proportionate and whether delays are managed and justified.
- Retraining is a legitimate action where the cause is a knowledge or skill gap. It is not automatically inadequate; explore whether it fits the established cause.

### 6. Implementation
- What evidence shows the actions were actually carried out (changed documents, training records, validation, design change records, supplier responses)?
- Implementation evidence shows the action happened; it does not by itself show the action worked.

### 7. Verification of effectiveness
- How was effectiveness defined *before* the check, and what measure, data or period was chosen and why?
- Was the check carried out as planned, and what did it show?
- Effectiveness means the action achieved its intended result to an appropriate, defined degree. It does **not** require a guarantee of zero recurrence, and there is no universal sample size or monitoring period.
- If the check failed or was inconclusive, what happened next?

### 8. Closure and feedback
- Who approved closure, on what evidence?
- How do CAPA outcomes feed risk management, management review, trend analysis and, where relevant, design or PMS?
- How is overall CAPA process performance monitored (e.g. ageing, recurrence, effectiveness check outcomes)?

## Typical areas worth exploring

These are areas to explore. They are not findings and do not presume failure. Each needs inspected evidence (the SOP or a supplied record) before it can become a nonconformity.

- Cause stated without supporting evidence, or restating the symptom.
- Effectiveness check missing, undefined in advance, or equated with implementation.
- Actions that do not follow from the stated cause.
- Inconsistent entry decisions for similar issues, or no rationale recorded.
- Containment not considering product already distributed.
- Repeat issues handled as unrelated single events.
- Changes made by a CAPA not assessed for impact on validation, risk or design.
- CAPA data not reaching management review.

## Framework notes

- **ISO 13485 / MDSAP:** corrective and preventive action are addressed as separate requirements, with expectations around documentation, proportionality to the effects of the nonconformity, and verification that action does not adversely affect regulatory requirements or device safety and performance. For MDSAP, jurisdiction-specific requirements (e.g. reporting) sit alongside ISO 13485 and must be kept distinct. Clause numbers and exact wording: see reference-status.md.
- **ISO 9001:** nonconformity and corrective action are addressed together; preventive action is not a separate requirement and is handled through risk-based thinking. Confirm the edition.
- **GMP:** deviation management, CAPA and change control are closely linked and expectations vary by regime and sector. Clarify the regime; do not import medical-device requirements.
- **FDA QMSR (21 CFR 820):** status and effective-date details are unverified (see reference-status.md); do not cite specifics as fact.

## Debrief reminders for CAPA

- Classify using SKILL.md's three categories only (nonconformity, observation/potential finding, preparation gap).
- A participant's description of a CAPA record is not an inspected record.
- Do not prescribe a root-cause technique, deadline or sample size as a requirement.
- Give indicative classifications for nonconformities only where justified, per grading.md.
